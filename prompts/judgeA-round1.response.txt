CANDIDATE 1 ATTACKS

A1, severity 4. Mechanism: C1 ships 0.58 gate, 45pct band, and cap 8 as defaults. Who breaks it: cross-domain users, multilingual teams, code versus prose workloads. Why it works: Jev calibration is not invariant across types, and locked constraint 3 forbids porting thresholds. Incentive: faster onboarding. Demo-day failure: labeled demo passes, live traffic false-admits because the threshold came from another type. Improvement: must-do per-task calibration wizard and refusal to use universal thresholds; nice-to-have threshold registry.

A2, severity 4. Mechanism: Multiple Jev gates on write and read, each 70-500ms, plus server and MCP. Who breaks it: interactive agents and high-write workloads. Why it works: Jev latency and context rot compound, and poisoning activation remains a risk. Incentive: full lifecycle completeness sells. Demo-day failure: slow response or timeout under load. Improvement: must-do code-side budget packer, async batching, caps, and kill switch; nice-to-have adaptive sampling.

A3, severity 5. Mechanism: Jev choice and noul literal reading on code-ordered pairs can be steered by injected memory content. Who breaks it: systems facing GhostWriter at ~98pct injection and ~60pct activation, or MemGhost at 87.5pct and 71.4pct. Why it works: adversarial steer risk plus no generation means Jev may read poisoned text literally. Incentive: attacker wants to overwrite memory. Demo-day failure: red team injects a supersede phrase and memory flips. Improvement: must-do provenance, signed writes, quarantine, code-owned supersede rules, and human override; nice-to-have anomaly detection.

A4, severity 3. Mechanism: One-command eval may tune and test on the same labeled data. Who breaks it: adopters trusting the score. Why it works: thresholds tuned per task need held-out data. Incentive: better benchmark numbers. Demo-day failure: leaderboard passes, live task fails. Improvement: must-do train/test split and contamination checks; nice-to-have third-party eval.

A5, severity 3. Mechanism: Honcho compatibility may drift from Honcho read semantics such as representation plus card first, then 40 or 60 summary and messages. Who breaks it: existing Honcho users migrating. Why it works: known waste includes over-iteration, prefetch duplication, uncapped injection, and count-based summaries. Incentive: compatibility claim. CANDIDATE 3 ATTACKS

A1, severity 5. Mechanism: New evidence triggers Jev supersede votes, and poisoned evidence can end leases. Who breaks it: systems facing GhostWriter at ~98pct injection and ~60pct activation, or MemGhost at 87.5pct and 71.4pct. Why it works: adversarial steer risk makes literal votes dangerous. Incentive: attacker deletes or masks facts. Demo-day failure: sidecar forgets a critical fact. Improvement: must-do multi-signal code policy, quarantine, and human override; nice-to-have delayed leases.

A2, severity 4. Mechanism: Lease ending removes or masks facts, and a ledger alone does not restore lost knowledge. Who breaks it: compliance teams and users. Why it works: forgetting has high blast radius. Incentive: clean memory. Demo-day failure: wrong deletion cannot be undone. Improvement: must-do soft-delete, versioning, and restore; nice-to-have TTL audit.

A3, severity 4. Mechanism: Apps write directly to the store and bypass the lease sidecar. Who breaks it: any non-MCP writer. Why it works: sidecar cannot enforce if it is not the sole gateway. Incentive: easy integration. Demo-day failure: stale fact persists despite lease expiry. Improvement: must-do store triggers or write-ahead interposition; nice-to-have SDK.

A4, severity 4. Mechanism: Leases need time, scope, and authority, but Jev has no math or dates. Who breaks it: temporal facts. Why it works: code must own math, and Jev should judge only literal contradiction or supersede. Incentive: simple vote model. Demo-day failure: wrong lease expiry. Improvement: must-do code-owned TTL and authority, Jev only literal contradiction; nice-to-have policy DSL.

A5, severity 3. Mechanism: Human override becomes a bottleneck when many leases are disputed. Who breaks it: high-contradiction domains. Why it works: every review needs a human. Incentive: safety. Demo-day failure: review backlog. Improvement: must-do tiered review; nice-to-have active learning.

A6, severity 4. Mechanism: One adversarial phrase forgets a key fact, or a stale fact is not forgotten. Who breaks it: demo audience. Why it works: forgetting is highly visible and trust-sensitive. Incentive: show control. Demo-day failure: trust collapse. Improvement: must-do scripted red-team and rollback; nice-to-have live ledger.
